                      Joining and leaving

                      Joining is one call per account. It approves Carbon Impact for two things: voting with the account, and claiming its rewards.

                      • Claims move at once. From the moment an account joins, Carbon Impact can claim any rewards it has not claimed yet, even those your own votes earned, and swaps and retires them like the rest. To keep them as they are, claim them on Hydrex before you join.
                      • Votes move at the next epoch, on Thursday at 00:00 UTC. From then, you can't vote with the account yourself, until the epoch after it leaves. Join on Wednesday at 23:00 UTC, and the votes move an hour later; join on Thursday at 10:00 UTC, and almost a week later.
                      • Nothing is transferred. The account and its HYDX stay in your wallet: neither approval lets Carbon Impact transfer them.
                      • Leaving is the same call with false, and works even if this page's checks fail. Claims stop at once: what Carbon Impact has not claimed, this epoch's included, is yours to claim on Hydrex. Your votes come back at the next epoch; until then they count for Carbon Impact.
                      Who controls what

                      Carbon Impact is Hydrex's: its code is in Hydrex's repository, and Hydrex lists it among its automations. A keeper, not Hydrex, now sends its votes and payouts.

                      Each week, it votes with the Earning Power of every account in it, and claims, swaps, retires and pays out each account. The pools, the swaps and how much to retire, up to the share set, are picked each week.

                      The admin key

                      It can change, at any time and for every member at once:

                      • the share retired, up to 100%;
                      • its fee on the payouts, up to 1%, and where the fee goes;
                      • the tokens it pays out in, and the routers it swaps through;
                      • the carbon credit retired, and the name and message on each retirement;
                      • any member's payout address;
                      • what joining approves, and who runs the votes and payouts.

                      It can also withdraw any tokens the automation holds.

                      What nobody can do

                      Move your account or your HYDX. Joining approves voting and claiming only, and leaving ends both.

                      How a change would show

                      Every change is a transaction from the admin key to the automation, on Basescan. This page reads the share retired, the fee and the name on the retirements each time it opens, and shows them as they are. It checks what joining approves, and turns joining off if that changed.

                          • Nobody controls AutoMaxi: it has no owner, no settings and no upgrade path. A new fee, treasury or price feed means a new deployment, and owners joining again.
                          • Hydrex can upgrade its Voter, VotingEscrow and reward contracts, so AutoMaxi is only as safe as they are.
                          • The owner of Hydrex's reward contracts can deposit a bribe, win the vote with it and take it back, or take any bribe's tokens.
                          How it picks its votes

                          Anyone can propose its votes. It takes one only if it expects it to earn at least $1 more in bribes, counting WETH, cbBTC, USDC and EURC at Chainlink's prices, and some trading fees in other tokens at their pool's average price over the last half hour. A week without a vote earns nothing.

                          • Chainlink's prices only steer its votes. If a feed goes stale, its token counts for nothing, and a worse vote can win while it lasts.
                          • Hydrex's pools price the trading fees in other tokens, and they too only steer its votes. Someone who moves a thin pool's price, or Hydrex, which can change how its pools report prices, can make a worse vote win. A pool's fees in other tokens never count for more than its fees in the four tokens above.

                              What the page checks

                              Before it offers swaps into an output, and every hour while it is open, the page checks on Base that:

                              • the RPC it reads from serves Base;
                              • each swapper runs the code above;
                              • it pays in its output token;
                              • its fee goes to the 2-of-3 Safe in the fee facts;
                              • its fee is 1%;
                              • and its exchanges are the twelve factories above, in that order.

                              If a check fails, swaps into that output stay off, and the page marks it here. Resetting an approval works.

                              How the routes are found

                              The swapper never picks a route, so the page does, from the factories. For each token, it tries the output straight, or through WETH, USDC or cbBTC, and HYDX for Hydrex's tokens. For each hop, it asks every factory for its pools of the two tokens: Uniswap v3's four fee tiers, v4's standard pools without hooks, and every tick spacing of each Slipstream. It quotes each with the swapper's own quote, keeps the best, net of gas, then runs the approvals and the swap through eth_simulateV1 before your wallet asks.

                              How a quote can differ from the swap

                              A quote prices each route against the pools as they are, so two routes through one pool are each priced as if first; the simulation catches that. Prices move between the quote and the swap, and Aero's classic pools add their tax above a tip of 1 gwei. The minimum caps the loss: a route that would pay less is skipped, and its token stays in your wallet.

                              Swapping without this page

                              On Basescan, call approve(spender, amount) on each token, with the swapper's address, then swap(routes, deadline, skipFailed) on the swapper, under Write Contract. Each route names the token, the amount, the least to receive after the 1%, and its hops: the exchange's number above, the pool, the token out, and Uniswap's fee and tick spacing.

                              For co-signers

                              A Safe transaction from this page approves each token for exactly what it sells, to the swapper of the output, then calls that swapper's swap, with a deadline a day later, so that a route that falls below its minimum while you sign is skipped. What it fetches, less 1%, comes back to the Safe. In Safe{Wallet}, check that each approval names one of the swappers above, and that the swap goes to the same one.

                              About

                              metaDEX Café is a page for looking after your Hydrex accounts and swapping tokens on Base, from your own wallet. It is independent: not Hydrex's, not Dromos's. It names no operator, and asks you to trust no one: what it shows is read from Base.

                              The official address

                              https://metadex.cafe/. Anyone can host a changed copy, for example one that approves another conduit, so use this address, and read what your wallet shows.

                              What it does not do

                              It has no server and stores nothing. It asks your wallet nothing until you pick it, and then only for transactions you confirm, never for signatures. Everything it shows comes from Base, through public RPCs: mainnet.base.org, base.drpc.org and base-rpc.publicnode.com. It never sells a token you did not tick, and shows each route before your wallet asks.

                              From a Safe

                              In Safe{Wallet}, add https://metadex.cafe/ as a custom Safe App. The page proposes one Safe transaction for all the calls, a swap's approvals included, and the Safe's owners sign it in Safe{Wallet}. Each product's "Check it" says what its calls do, for the co-signers.

                              If this page goes away

                              Your accounts stay as they are, and the automations go on. Leaving is one call on Hydrex's VotingEscrow, which any contract interface, such as Basescan's, can make. Your tokens stay in your wallet. Approvals can be reset on each token's contract, and the swappers called from Basescan.

                              How the figures are read

                              • Prices: each token's average over the half hour before the epoch ended, in the deepest of Hydrex's pools that pairs it with USDC, WETH or cbBTC; WETH and cbBTC at Chainlink's ETH / USD and BTC / USD feeds; USDC as a dollar. A pool counts only if it holds at least $250 of each of its tokens, and ten times the value it prices. A token without one counts for nothing. Dollars are marked ≈.
                              • Earning Power is shown as on Hydrex: 1.3 times the votes the contracts count.
                              • Fees and incentives: what votes earned counts both. Hydrex's own weekly figures leave out the incentives, so they are lower.
                              • Compared with the average, not with the best pool: a pool's yield falls as votes move to it, and the average is what Earning Power actually earned. One epoch is noisy, as incentives change every week.
                              • Paid: an epoch counts as paid once claims since it ended took more than half of what its rewards were worth, so that a claim of a few small rewards does not. A receipt's dollars are at the prices of the epoch's end before the payout.
                              • What waits to be claimed is a floor: the page reads an account's rewards in the pools its automation voted for last epoch, and no others.
                              • Swap quotes are the swapper's own quote, route by route, then the approvals and the swap run together through eth_simulateV1. A token is ticked when its quote is worth twice its gas, at Base's gas price and Chainlink's ETH / USD.
                              • The price check: a quote more than 2% under the page's own price for the token, its average over the last half hour in Hydrex's pools as above, says so, with both figures.
